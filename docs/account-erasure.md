# Account erasure and retention proposal — MAN-117

**Status: proposed design, not implemented deletion or accepted retention policy.** This is `palmy-erasure/1-draft1`, based on `main` at `4132be9` and the [current MAN-117 acceptance criteria](https://linear.app/skyholding/issue/MAN-117/specify-account-erasure-and-retention-boundaries). It covers FR-21, FR-22 and NFR-04, with the financial integrity and authorization requirements in NFR-02. None of the future endpoints, jobs or acceptance scenarios below run today. No recovery protocol, runtime grant, immutable-ledger trigger or deployed data changes in this task.

Authority: [delivery map](delivery.md), [security boundary](security.md), [implemented v1 protocol](protocol.md), [generated OpenAPI](../contracts/openapi.json), `api/migrations/0001_identity_finance.sql`, `api/src/{auth,db,finance,lib}.rs`, and the separate [credential lifecycle proposal](credential-lifecycle.md). Historical Flutter/identity-provider plans are not this implementation. Professional protocol review, retention-owner decisions, operational evidence and accepted follow-up tickets remain external gates; this proposal does not satisfy them by existing.

## Meaning of the action

`Lock` clears local access material and attempts to revoke one session. Revoking sessions or devices blocks some future access. Neither action deletes account records, readable money history, exports or backups. Deleting the encrypted profile alone also leaves readable finance and stable owner links. Losing a profile key is not proof of erasure: copies may survive on devices, and Palmy never held that key. Finance is readable to the service and may itself identify people.

The proposed action is **close this personal account and erase the enumerated Palmy-controlled copies**, with a receipt that distinguishes access closure, online-store removal, remaining approved retention, backup expiry and external-copy limits. No claim covers a recipient's independently saved export, a screenshot, a password-manager recovery copy or an independently controlled provider record. Shared-household deletion is a separate future action; account ownership does not authorize deleting another member's records.

## Decisions requiring acceptance

No named privacy owner has accepted these decisions. The project must record an accountable privacy/product decision-maker and the acceptance reference for each row; specialist legal review is needed where actual deployment jurisdiction, controller obligations, contracts or required record retention affect a decision. This document supplies no jurisdictional retention rule or legal conclusion.

| ID | Decision / engineering recommendation and rationale | Required acceptance evidence |
|---|---|---|
| ER-D01 | Personal account closure is irreversible after confirmation. Preview can be abandoned; there is no post-confirmation grace period that secretly keeps login working. Fewer reversible states reduce accidental resurrection. | Product/privacy acceptance of scope, cancellation point and Indonesian wording. |
| ER-D02 | Purge all eligible personal readable ledger and idempotency copies; keep ordinary journals immutable until the privileged purge boundary. Do not retain all finance indefinitely merely because it is called an audit trail. | Approved data-category retention matrix; any required exception names its fields, reason, access role, finite review/expiry condition and responsible role. |
| ER-D03 | Require current root authority plus fresh purpose-bound confirmation. A bearer token, device controller, household owner of a different account, email claim or support agent is insufficient. | Acceptance of root-loss consequences and independent review of the step-up extension. A separately reviewed v1 adapter or explicit v2 upgrade route is required for existing accounts. |
| ER-D04 | No erasure activation until actual backup, WAL, replica, restore-copy and provider retention maxima are known and enforceable. Use named policy parameters, not unapproved invented day counts. | Signed-off maximum durations and deletion SLOs; operational owner accepts alert/escalation and restore suppression obligations. |
| ER-D05 | Retain a bounded minimal receipt while remaining work can be tracked; keep a separately protected keyed suppression marker while any restore or late producer can reintroduce the account. Recommend never reusing a closed account UUID. | Receipt expiry and suppression retention accepted. Permanent UUID non-reuse may require an indefinitely retained keyed retirement marker; this is pseudonymous retained metadata, not anonymous data. An alternative identifier-retirement design needs review before that marker can expire. |
| ER-D06 | Approved retained financial records move to a segregated, access-restricted retention domain with no product access and explicit per-category expiry. A hold is an exception, not proof of erasure. | Hold authority, evidence, review/expiry and audit access approved. If no valid category policy exists, refuse confirmation rather than silently invent a hold or destroy required records. |
| ER-D07 | Automatic initial scope is personal accounts only. Shared ledgers, household transfer/last-owner rules and provider-controlled copies require dedicated policy and adapters before enablement. | Ownership/disclosure decisions and provider agreements reviewed for each enabled feature. |
| ER-D08 | No email/support identity override when root proof is unavailable. A support request can receive an explanation; it cannot become cryptographic account authorization. | Approved lost-key request policy, abuse controls and any required non-cryptographic process designed separately without promising an existing bypass. |

Suggested engineering constants for the future implementation are a 120-second one-use proof challenge and a 10-minute preview lifetime, matching the lifecycle proposal's interaction windows. They are reviewable technical defaults, not retention periods. No approved values yet exist for `active_purge_slo`, `provider_resolution_slo`, `backup_max_age`, `wal_max_age`, `restored_copy_max_age`, `receipt_retention`, `audit_retention` or `suppression_retention`. Configuration must have validated explicit values and a versioned acceptance reference; missing policy disables confirmation. The proposed lifecycle receipt minimum in MAN-115 does not automatically become erasure policy.

## Preview, fresh proof and confirmation

This design depends on a reviewed common account authorization fence and lifecycle proof implementation. For v2 accounts it proposes adding **fixed** purposes `erasure_preview` and `erasure_confirm` to the allowlist in MAN-115; they are absent today. Reuse its strict encoding, JCS canonicalization, one-use PostgreSQL challenges and Ed25519 proof binding. A new purpose must receive cross-platform vectors and protocol review before implementation. A v1 login signature is never accepted as an erasure proof. This draft does not invent a v1 erasure transcript: the migration/adapter decision ER-D03 is an activation blocker for current v1 accounts.

1. The unlocked client chooses **Hapus akun**, shows the local account context and requests a preview using current root proof. A v2 device session can explain the feature but cannot confirm it. The recovery secret/private key never leaves the device. Existing root material can sign only after an explicit user confirmation; a UI PIN/biometric alone cannot grant server root authority.
2. The client creates an operation UUID and a random 32-byte **receipt credential**, retaining it locally in memory. Its SHA-256 digest is included in the preview request and eventual signed confirmation. Before destructive confirmation the user explicitly saves the receipt credential and operation ID outside Palmy, or explicitly accepts losing later status access. It is a status-only capability, not a recovery key. No automatic browser persistence, URL token, email delivery or analytics. The server never returns an account credential in this flow.
3. The preview-request operation payload is exactly `{kind:"erasure_preview", protocol:"2", account_id, operation_id, scope:"personal_account", receipt_credential_hash}`; the root proof binds it and the current lifecycle revisions. The server captures a consistent owner-scoped inventory and versioned policy snapshot. The preview includes the account UUID, operation UUID, expiry, scope `personal_account`, authority protocol/root and credential revisions, session epoch, profile/ledger revision, erasure inventory revision, policy version/digest, exact per-class counts, disposition (`purge`, `retain_until`, `external_not_controlled`, `not_present`), outstanding jobs/provider actions, retention reasons and deadlines, restore/backup horizon and closure consequences. No profile plaintext, descriptions, names, secrets, provider identifiers or complete financial rows are copied into the preview. Zero means inventoried absence; unknown inventory is explicit and blocks confirmation.
4. Show **will erase**, **will remain until**, **outside Palmy control** and **access ends immediately** in Indonesian. For example: `Akses akun akan ditutup. Data pada cadangan tetap menunggu jadwal penghapusan yang ditampilkan.` Dates come from the accepted policy and actual inventory; never display a fabricated promise. Offer export before closure as a separate future owner-authorized workflow; a pending export is not a reason to bypass the erasure fence. Require an unchecked explicit irreversible-action acknowledgement and a final `Hapus akun ini` action; do not rely on a colored button or typing a phrase as authorization. Provide accessible focus, labels, error recovery and native back behavior.
5. Only after the owner reviews the exact preview, obtain a fresh `erasure_confirm` challenge for its canonical hash and current revisions. Confirmation's exact operation payload is `{kind:"account_erasure", protocol:"2", account_id, operation_id, expected:{root_generation, credential_revision, session_epoch, profile_revision, ledger_revision, erasure_inventory_revision}, preview_hash, policy_hash, receipt_credential_hash, irreversible_acknowledged:true, receipt_saved_or_loss_acknowledged:true}`. Use decimal-string counters, canonical UUIDs/base64url and strict unknown/duplicate-field rejection as in MAN-115. All fields are inside the proof's payload hash; actor must be the current root. The UI verifies the challenge purpose/hash and does not sign arbitrary server-supplied text.
6. The server consumes/verifies the one-use proof and obtains the exclusive account fence. In the commit transaction it rechecks root authority, every expected revision, preview expiry, policy and retention/ownership disposition, receipt digest and acknowledgements. Any relevant data, jobs, shared ownership, policy or key change returns a conflict and requires a new preview and fresh proof. Do not keep a database lock open while the user reviews. Expired/invalid attempts do not close the account.
7. Before closure commit, persist the validated operation's minimal suppression intent in the independently protected registry, with a bounded dependency timeout and immutable operation identity. Then commit closure exactly once: transition `active → erasure_pending`, advance the session/security fence, invalidate all sessions, auth/lifecycle challenges and prepared credential changes, seal the purge manifest, and insert durable erasure work and receipt rows. Confirmation returns **202 accepted/access closed**, not `erased`, only after both durable intent and database closure exist. Failure before database commit rolls back account/domain changes; a separately persisted intent may remain unresolved and is reconciled conservatively as described below. Receipt delivery failure after commit leaves closure effective; it is resolved through status, not by logging back in.

OWASP recommends separating sensitive-operation authorization from ordinary sign-in, binding the authorization to the reviewed operation, checking it at execution and limiting its lifetime/reuse. This is the technical basis for the preview/confirmation split; it is not a claim that Palmy already implements it. [OWASP Transaction Authorization Cheat Sheet](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Transaction_Authorization_Cheat_Sheet.html).

### Proposed API surface and retry behavior

Paths below are planning contracts only; do not add them to the running OpenAPI until implemented and verified. Preserve the current RFC 9457 error envelope and `Cache-Control: no-store`; redact receipt credentials like session tokens.

| Proposed operation | Authorization and result |
|---|---|
| `POST /api/v2/lifecycle/challenges` with fixed erasure purpose | Existing reviewed lifecycle root-proof challenge rules; bounded, uniform unknown-account shape and abuse limits. |
| `POST /api/v2/account-erasure/previews` | Fresh root `erasure_preview` proof; 201 immutable, short-lived scope/policy snapshot. At most one current preview per account; a newer one expires the older. Preview does not disable access. |
| `POST /api/v2/account-erasure/confirmations` | Fresh root `erasure_confirm` proof; 202 closure receipt. Operation UUID is durable idempotency identity; same UUID with changed canonical proposal is 409. No bearer-only or device-proof alternate. |
| `POST /api/v2/account-erasure/status` | Operation UUID in body plus `Authorization: ErasureReceipt <credential>`; constant-time digest verification, explicit status-only middleware. 200 minimal state/receipt; invalid, pruned or unknown credentials receive the same bounded 404 shape. It cannot return inventory, finance, profile, account keys or mutate state. |

Use 400 for malformed/unknown fields, 401 for invalid/expired/reused root proof, 403 for an authenticated insufficient actor, 409 for stale revisions/policy/inventory or changed idempotency content, 410 for an expired preview after authorization, 429 for bounded rate limits and 503 for unavailable required dependencies. Errors disclose no foreign-account inventory. Status credentials are separately random, never derived from a root, account UUID or receipt ID; only their digest is stored. They expire with the accepted receipt policy, cannot be refreshed into account access, and require review and negative tests as a new limited credential surface.

A dropped confirmation reply is **unknown outcome**. With the saved receipt credential, query the same operation: `not found` before confirmation is not proof that a concurrent request cannot still commit. The client must resolve/retry the same operation under fresh authorization while active, or poll its receipt; it must not create a second deletion or claim cancellation from a timeout. A completed operation returns its same closure result through status even though root/session authority has been erased. A receipt credential cannot authorize or replay confirmation. If both credential and reply are lost after closure, there is no email recovery path. A prepared preview expires without closing the account.

## Fence, immutable ledger and durable work

The current `db::authenticate` precedes domain transactions and checks only an unexpired session; adding a flag to the account table alone is insufficient. Implement the MAN-115 common lock/fence contract for **every** v1/v2 route, authentication issuance, cached read, profile mutation and future worker before erasure is enabled. Protected work acquires the shared account fence and rechecks live status/epoch inside its transaction; closure uses the exclusive fence. Order locks as account security, ledger owner, then domain IDs. No erasure flag may be supplied by the caller to bypass RLS or triggers.

A write that acquired the shared fence first may finish before closure; its changed revision invalidates the preview. Closure that wins first prevents subsequent old-authority writes, sign-in, profile edits and cached reads. No new journal can commit after the successful closure commit. Already-authorized response bytes can arrive afterward and cannot be recalled. Cache fills must finish within the guarded operation, or use a second guarded pre-write check; today's summary code fills after committing its database read, which must be changed before this guarantee is claimed.

Recommended additive state (not migrations):

| State | Purpose and minimization |
|---|---|
| Account fence extension | `active/erasure_pending/retention_only`, monotonic authorization epoch and inventory revision; common serialization point, including all legacy paths. A fully purged account has no normal account row. |
| `erasure_previews` | Owner, operation ID, exact hashes/revisions, bounded class counts/dispositions and expiry. Purge expired/superseded previews; never financial rows or plaintext profile. |
| `erasure_operations` | Operation ID, owner mapping only while work needs it, immutable proposal/policy hash, state/times, receipt digest/expiry, bounded class progress and error codes. Scrub owner mapping and manifest details after last dependent purge; no credentials or returned financial JSON. |
| `erasure_work` | Transactional owner-scoped tasks, attempt/lease/checkpoint and category IDs; database outbox survives crashes. Child deletion IDs are internal, access-controlled and purged once reconciled. No arbitrary SQL/table names from clients. |
| Suppression/retirement registry | Keyed digest of account UUID with key version and closure sequence; enough to reject resurrection and duplicate closure. Store separately from recoverable application backups; restrict lookup and key access. Hashing does not make this anonymous. |
| Approved retention domain | Only explicitly excepted fields, retention policy/reason, restricted purpose, review/expiry and provenance. No product reads, active sessions or general support browsing. Its copies/backups remain in the manifest until their own expiry. |

`journals` and `entries` currently reject UPDATE/DELETE and runtime SQL grants exclude financial DELETE. Preserve that normal-path protection. A separately authenticated worker with a **bounded stored procedure or equally reviewed least-privilege role** may purge only an already-fenced account and sealed manifest, with approved retention disposition. It must not receive table ownership, BYPASSRLS, a general trigger-disable command, arbitrary owner parameters without manifest binding, or a reusable session setting that a runtime caller can copy. Scope any trigger exception to that worker identity and operation/owner checked inside the transaction; pin SQL search path and revoke PUBLIC execution. The implementation must prove ordinary runtime connections still cannot mutate posted history, including under forged session settings and pooled-connection reuse.

For the current personal graph, delete eligible idempotency copies, entries, journals, wallets, ledger state, profile, remaining auth metadata and account in dependency order, in one transaction for a bounded account or checkpointed batches behind a permanent closed fence for a large account. No batch may expose a partly deleted ledger through product APIs. Do not create a reversal to make erasure possible: it adds history instead of erasing it. Failure leaves the account closed and work resumable; rollback must never reopen it or revive secrets. An unsupported or unknown domain stops completion with an explicit blocked category. Counts/digests alone do not prove absence: reconcile by owner and object inventories using a privileged, controlled verifier.

The worker reconciles the already durable suppression intent with the closure commit before purging stores. A registry outage before intent persistence prevents confirmation; an outage after closure leaves the account closed and work resumable, never erased by assertion. Delete all cache revisions and reconcile a second pass after in-flight reads are drained. Retry every deletion idempotently: an already absent object is success, an authorization error is failure, a provider timeout is unknown outcome. Repeated worker crashes, dead letters and partial provider outcomes are visible as `blocked` with category-level codes and operational escalation; a retry limit must not silently mark success or reopen access.

Future domain workers must check the account fence at enqueue, execution, commit and delivery. Cancel queued/retry/dead-letter work, invalidate leases and reject late callbacks. A provider call already dispatched before closure may finish externally; record that unresolved disposition and request erasure/reconciliation. Never let a delayed webhook create a fresh account/mapping when its prior owner is suppressed. Downloads need an authorization-aware gateway or a revocation/expiry scheme whose remaining lifetime is shown; a previously issued raw object-store URL may remain usable until expiry and cannot be advertised as instantly revoked.

## Backups, restore suppression and completion evidence

Logical SQL deletion does not sanitize underlying media or eliminate an older backup. NIST SP 800-88 Rev. 2 defines media sanitization around making access to target data infeasible and describes a program of appropriate controls. It does not establish Palmy's retention policy or make a database DELETE a sanitization certificate. [NIST SP 800-88 Rev. 2](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/88/r2/final).

Before activation, enumerate each backup family and actual copy, encryption/key ownership, maximum age, WAL range, replicas, provider snapshot rules and restore destinations. Compute remaining-copy deadlines from **actual backup creation/expiry evidence**, including copies made during delayed purging; `closure time + backup_max_age` is insufficient if a later backup still contains the account. A retention exception can extend the horizon and must be reflected in status. Purge/suppress new online copies promptly; do not extend old backup retention by repeatedly copying it into a fresh backup. A missed expiry moves the operation to `blocked`, not a fabricated completion timestamp.

The authoritative suppression registry and its recovery evidence must survive restoring an older application database. The external intent is written **before** closure can commit, avoiding a lost-database/unsent-outbox window; a database outbox then reconciles committed results and progress, with monitored sequence/watermark lag. An intent with unknown database outcome causes restore quarantine for that account. Resolve it using durable authoritative commit/abort evidence, never merely the absence of an erasure row in an old snapshot. A verified pre-commit abort can release a tentative intent; a confirmed closure can never be undone this way. This cross-store protocol requires fault injection at each boundary and explicit operational acceptance.

Disaster recovery restores into quarantine with public API, worker dispatch, provider callbacks, exports and backup propagation disabled. Obtain the latest independently protected suppression registry, validate its integrity and continuity, replay **all closures after the restored snapshot** (including pending/blocked erasures) and quarantine unresolved intents, purge suppressed rows/caches/auth/job artifacts, and reconcile negative queries before any traffic or exports are enabled. If the registry is missing, stale or unverifiable, restore stays closed; a manual override cannot count as an erasure-safe restore. Restoration must not silently revive sessions/challenges, reenroll devices, refetch deleted provider records or register the same retired UUID. Test restoration from a snapshot that predates confirmation, not only from a post-deletion dump.

Use separate evidence dimensions, rather than one misleading `deleted=true`:

| Dimension | Receipt meaning |
|---|---|
| `access_closed_at` | Common authorization fence committed. No claim about stored copies. |
| `active_store_state` | `pending/purged/retained/blocked`; every Palmy-controlled live DB/cache/object/job/replica adapter reports and reconciliation agrees. `retained` names approved category/reason/deadline. |
| `provider_state` | `not_applicable/pending/confirmed/limited/blocked`; a disconnect cannot set `confirmed`. Independent provider copies appear in limitations. |
| `backup_state` | `pending/expired_or_sanitized/retained/blocked`; actual known-copy expiry/destruction and a restore-suppression drill supply evidence. Encryption-key destruction counts only for reviewed independently scoped keys and all key copies; current client profile encryption does not erase readable finance. |
| `completion` | `in_progress/complete_with_declared_retention/complete_in_scope/blocked`. `complete_in_scope` requires every in-scope class and backup resolved, with only the disclosed minimal metadata and externally controlled limitations remaining. `complete_with_declared_retention` requires all non-retained work resolved and explicit approved exceptions with enforced deadlines; it is not full erasure. Pending or failed work cannot be relabeled a retention exception. |

Receipt fields are operation UUID, proposal/policy hashes, state/timestamps, bounded category status/reason codes, approved remaining deadlines, backup horizon and externally controlled limitations, plus `receipt_expires_at`. They exclude owner ID, profile/finance payloads, amounts, exact object names, device/public keys, IPs and provider identifiers. The temporary worker mapping remains restricted until purge completes; the final receipt needs no account link. A minimal audit event records operation ID, transition, policy version, result code and service role, not the raw proof or receipt credential. These are still potentially linkable records and require the ER-D05 retention decision. Expired receipts return the same status response as unknown credentials. Aggregate service metrics may count operations without identity labels.

A receipt reports what the service verified; it is not independently verifiable proof against a malicious operator. Closure cannot retract external copies or prove what an offline device retained. The UI must continue showing `Penghapusan sedang diproses` or a specific retained/blocked status until the corresponding evidence exists. Clearing the local recovery key is optional user cleanup after a saved receipt, never the server's proof of erasure. Backgrounding/locking cancels pending UI operations; a late callback cannot reopen the account or silently submit confirmation. Offline confirmation is unavailable; queued destructive actions are not replayed automatically on reconnect.

## Delivery and review gates

[Erasure cases](../contracts/erasure-cases.json) are declarative future scenarios, **not executable tests or passing runtime evidence**. [Published follow-ups](erasure-followups.json), MAN-124–136, split policy, schema, API, workers, client flows and fault/restore tests. Scopes remain unestimated until capacity and prerequisites are known. These proposals are in Backlog under MAN-81 with explicit blocking links to MAN-117: policy decisions unblock design acceptance, while implementation children depend on accepted MAN-117 and MAN-115 foundations as applicable. MAN-126 also depends on MAN-116's common session fencing. Implementation does not become a prerequisite for completing the design issue. Engineering review and publication do not replace policy acceptance, specialist review or assignment of the follow-ups.

Before enabling even a personal-account pilot: record ER-D01–08 decisions, review purpose-bound proof and receipt credential design, implement and test all-route fencing (including v1 bypasses), preserve normal immutable/RLS grants, prove actual two-connection race outcomes, complete web/Android/iOS lifecycle tests, instrument all live-store/backup adapters, run a restore from a pre-erasure snapshot with stale/missing-registry negative cases, audit logs for synthetic sensitive markers, and obtain independent security/privacy and operational acceptance. Future file/provider/household features reopen the inventory and cannot inherit a fictitious `not_present` disposition. Deploy disabled/additive components first; rollback leaves closed accounts closed and restarts resumable work, never restores a deleted account to active.
